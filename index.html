The Sacred Sequence

A Tamil wedding unfolds like a kolam: one careful line after another. Walk through each ritual, and tap to take part.

  1. Days before
  2. ௧

    பந்தக்கால் முகூர்த்தம்

    Panda Kaal Muhurtham

    The very first ritual. Our families plant a bamboo pole at the venue, smeared with turmeric and dotted with kumkum and crowned with mango leaves. It asks the gods for a celebration that goes ahead without any hitch.

  3. The evening before · 26 Nov
  4. ௨

    ஜானவாசம்

    Janavasam

    The groom's grand arrival. A procession of music, lights and loved ones brings him in a garlanded car, traditionally stopping at a nearby temple for blessings before reaching the marriage hall.

  5. Muhurtham morning · 27 Nov
  6. ௩

    காசி யாத்திரை

    Kasi Yatra

    The groom playfully announces he is giving up worldly life and sets off for Kasi with an umbrella, a hand fan and a walking stick. The bride's father stops him on the way and offers his daughter's hand, persuading him to choose family life instead.

  7. 0 / 3
    ௪

    மாலை மாற்றல்

    Maalai Maatral

    The couple exchange heavy flower garlands three times, often lifted high on the shoulders of cousins who make it a playful contest. Each exchange is a promise that two hearts are now one.

  8. ௫

    ஊஞ்சல்

    Oonjal

    The couple sit together on a decorated wooden swing while the women of the family sing the oonjal songs. They circle coloured rice balls around the couple and toss them away to ward off the evil eye, then sprinkle milk on the couple's feet.

  9. 0 / 3
    ௬

    மாங்கல்ய தாரணம்

    Mangalya Dharanam

    The moment everything has been building towards. At the auspicious hour, with the bride in her nine-yard koorai pudavai, the groom ties the thaali on its sacred turmeric thread. He ties the first knot and his sister ties the other two. The Getti Melam drums thunder so that no inauspicious sound is heard.

    Māngalyam tantunānena mama jīvana hetunā
    kanthe badhnāmi subhage tvam jīva śaradām śatam

    “This is the sacred thread, essential to my life. I tie it around your neck, O auspicious one. May you live happily for a hundred years.”

  10. 0 / 7
    ௭

    சப்தபதி

    Saptapadi

    Holding the bride's right hand, the groom leads her through seven steps around the sacred fire, one vow for each step. After the seventh step they are friends for life.

    Sakhā saptapadī bhava

    “With seven steps, become my friend.”

  11. ௮

    அம்மி மிதித்தல்

    Ammi Midhithal

    The groom gently places the bride's right foot on the ammi, a granite grinding stone, and slips silver metti onto her toes. It is a wish that their marriage stays as steady as stone. Afterwards the couple look up at Arundhati, the star of devotion.

  12. …and they lived happily, ever after

மரபுகள்

Silk, Sweets & Sound

Woven in Kanchipuram silk, shaped out of sweets and carried along by nadaswaram music.

கூறைப் புடவை

Koorai Pudavai

A nine-yard silk saree, traditionally deep red or maroon with heavy gold zari. The groom's family gifts it, and the bride changes into it just before the thaali is tied.

பஞ்சகச்சம்

Panchakacham

The groom's nine-yard veshti, wrapped in five distinctive tucks and pleats and paired with a silk angavastram over the shoulder.

பருப்புத் தேங்காய்

Paruppu Thengai

A pair of tall, glittering cones packed with sweets such as laddu, Mysore pak or sugared dal. Exchanged between the families, they stand for abundance and a sweet life together.

நாதஸ்வரம் & தவில்

Nadaswaram & Thavil

The double-reed nadaswaram carries the melody while the barrel-shaped thavil keeps the rhythm, and together they play right through the ceremony.

A Name Hidden in Mehandi

The night before the wedding, intricate maruthaani designs cover the bride's hands. Tradition hides the groom's name somewhere in the design, and the groom has to find it.
